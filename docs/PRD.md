# 1. Brand Overview

Cheer the Kids is an NGO focused on giving children hope, restoring joy and helping young people develop the self-belief to pursue their dreams and aspirations. The organisation currently works across Southwest Nigeria, with documented activities in Lagos and Kwara, and has a long-term view toward expansion.

Current impact: over 1,300 children reached through 7 school/community outreaches.

# 2. Brand Purpose, Mission & Vision

## Mission

“To provide programs and activities that enhance happiness and instill self-belief in children.”

## Vision

“To ensure that every child experiences joy and has the hope to believe in their dreams and aspirations.”

## Brand purpose

To create positive experiences, encouragement and opportunities that help children keep their spark alive and believe that their circumstances do not have to define their future.

The brand should feel:

# 5. Logo Guidelines

The supplied logo is the primary visual identifier for Cheer the Kids. The logo combines purple and gold abstract forms around a blue central figure/shape, with a blue wordmark.


## Logo concept direction

The visual language suggests connection, care, protection, joy and the centrality of the child. The purple and gold forms create a protective/embracing relationship around the blue central element.

## Clear space

Maintain clear space around the logo equal to at least the diameter/height of the small circular element in the symbol. Do not place text, photographs or other graphic elements inside this protected area.

## Do

- Use the approved logo artwork without distortion.

- Maintain the original colour relationships.

- Use sufficient contrast against the background.

- Use the full logo where brand recognition is important.

## Do not

- Stretch or compress the logo.

- Rotate the logo.

- Change individual logo colours arbitrarily.

- Add unapproved shadows, outlines or effects.

- Place the logo on visually busy areas without sufficient contrast.

- Recreate the wordmark using a different font.

# 7. Typography

The exact font used in the supplied logo should not be inferred or substituted. The following is a recommended supporting type system for the website, documents and social media until the original brand font specifications are confirmed.

| **Level**               | **Recommended font**       | **Use**                                                |
|-------------------------|----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| Primary headings        | Poppins SemiBold/Bold      | Page titles, major statements and campaign headlines   |
| Body text               | Nunito Sans Regular/Medium | Website copy, reports, descriptions and long-form text |
| Buttons / labels        | Poppins Medium/SemiBold    | CTAs, navigation and short labels                      |

Typography principle: keep text highly readable, friendly and modern. Avoid excessive decorative fonts.

# 8. Photography & Visual Direction

Photography should show children and volunteers experiencing the work rather than simply posing for documentation.

- Prioritise authentic moments: laughter, games, learning, creativity, conversation and participation.

- Show children as active participants, not objects of pity.

- Use natural expressions and documentary-style images where possible.

- Use bright, well-lit photography with real environments.

- Capture volunteers, teachers, partners and communities as part of the story.

- Maintain appropriate consent and safeguarding procedures for all identifiable children.

## Visual storytelling formula

Challenge → Engagement → Experience → Human connection → Hope → Outcome

# 9. Graphic & Digital Design Direction

- Use rounded or flowing shapes that echo the logo's embracing forms.

- Use circles and curved forms as recurring graphic motifs.

- Use purple, gold and blue as accents rather than overwhelming every layout.

- Allow generous white space.

- Use photographs prominently.

- Use simple icons and friendly illustrations where helpful.

- Keep layouts energetic but organised.

- Avoid overly corporate, gloomy or charity-pity visual styles.

## Recommended digital style

Soft, friendly, bold and modern — combining documentary photography with clean graphic shapes and strong calls to action.

# 11. Product Overview

The Cheer the Kids website will serve as the organisation's official digital home. It will communicate the organisation's mission, demonstrate impact, document past projects, recruit volunteers, facilitate donations, attract partners and provide a trusted point of contact for schools and the public.

# 12. Problem Statement

Children experiencing difficult family, economic and social circumstances may lose confidence, enthusiasm and hope for the future. At the same time, organisations supporting these children need public trust, volunteers, funding and partnerships to sustain and expand their work. The website will provide a central platform for Cheer the Kids to communicate its work and mobilise the people and resources required to increase its impact.

# 19. Programmes

## 19.1 School Outreaches

School outreaches are designed to engage children around purpose, self-belief and keeping their spark alive.

- Group conversations on purpose and self-belief.

- Games and interactive activities.

- Fun videos and creative engagement.

- Prayer with the children.

Volunteer training is intended for young Nigerians in tertiary institutions and graduates of tertiary institutions, with professionals also eligible to volunteer.

Applicants are expected to complete a screening test sent by email and participate in training before deployment.

# 27. Child Safeguarding & Privacy

Because Cheer the Kids works with children, safeguarding is a core product requirement.

- Establish a child safeguarding policy.

- Establish photography/video/media consent procedures.

- Define rules for children's names, images, videos and personal stories.

- Limit collection of unnecessary personal information.

- Protect volunteer, donor and contact-form data.

- Restrict administrative access to submitted information.

- Publish an appropriate privacy notice.

- Review all child-facing content before publication.
